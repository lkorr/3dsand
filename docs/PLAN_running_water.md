# Running water that sleeps (2026-10-16)

Owner ask, verbatim in spirit: *rivers, the ocean and large bodies of water
have essentially infinite volume; running paths of voxels that do the same
thing over and over should fall asleep while still looking like they push water
down the path; water should run a little vertically, even over waterfalls,
without tanking performance.*

Owner decisions (asked before starting): waterfalls are a **held voxel sheet**
(real water voxels, swimmable, reacting); persistent player-made streams
**auto-convert to held** (phase 2); river bake becomes **sloped + rapids +
falls**; work **lands on main when green**.

## The problem, precisely

A reach-1 CA cannot let a falling surface rest. Every step of a slope is a
lateral or diagonal move `stepLiquid` will make; feed the top and drain the
bottom (the reservoir already does both) and the river is a conveyor that never
sleeps — CLAUDE.md rule 2. That is why the old bake made rivers a STAIRCASE OF
STILL POOLS (flat reaches, weirs, "waterfalls drawn as a step"), and why
`PLAN_water_master.md` §5 named the conveyor a non-goal.

Simulating the motion is unaffordable. Faking it with a separate non-voxel
river breaks everything that reads voxels (swimming, fire, buoyancy, stains,
digging). The answer here is a third thing: **the voxels are real and at rest,
and the CA is told that a river at its surface is already where it is going.**

## Phase 1 — HELD WATER (landed)

### 1. The surface in eighths (the still table)

`worldgen.wgsl cols` writes one record per window column (binding 58): the
authored surface of a map lake / river / the sea. The surface is now **T8, in
eighths**: full cells to `(T8 - 1) >> 3`, the top cell holding the rest.
Lakes and the sea are whole-voxel (`8 level + 8`); rivers carry a ramp.

### 2. The held rule (`sim_step.wgsl`, HELD WATER)

> A cell **at rest** in a held column — `8 y + f <= T8(column)` — never sends
> liquid sideways or diagonally into a cell of **another held column** (same
> liquid) that is **at or over** that column's own target.

Held water does not pour into held water that is already full. Nothing else
changes:

* a held column **under** its target (a crater, a scoop, a gap in a curtain)
  takes water from its held neighbours like any water; the neighbours that gave
  it are then under *their* targets and the reservoir refills them;
* a held cell beside a column that is **not held** (a dug bank) spills into it
  like any water — a **breach**, fed forever;
* water **over** a target (a bucket, a splash, a breach's stream coming back)
  is not at rest; it flows as water and the reservoir takes it back.

The reservoir (stage 5) now works in eighths, and its **deficit** branches
(TAKE, GROW) fire on every visit and fill the cell to its target in one write.
A TAKE is also tried FIRST (stage 0): a free-surface held cell under its target
is refilled before it may move, so a river cell beside a breach, or an adopted
stream column feeding the next one, hands on what the reservoir behind it
supplies instead of whatever was left. The **excess** branch (GIVE) keeps its
roll (`sim.stillWaterRelax`/1024, one eighth), so a splash still runs out
before it is gone. `stillWaterRelax = 0` switches the whole feature off (it is
`lake-settle`'s control arm).

Rule 1: pure reads of reach-1 cells and the still table (a function of seed,
map and column, plus phase 2's words, written in tick order). Rule 2: the rule
only ever *refuses* a move; an undisturbed held river has no move left and
sleeps.

`canFlowAnywhere` and `bridgeLevel` make the same test in the same places
(`heldRest`, `heldBlocks`, `heldBlocksAt`) — the mirror must agree or a chunk
pins awake or sleeps with work left.

### 3. The bake (`worldwater.cpp` step 8, `riverbake.js` the port)

The staircase S (running min, `stepVox`) is unchanged. Then every step that
drops at most `kRiverWaterfall` (20 voxels, 2 m) is **soft**: the surface ramps
down to it linearly in arc, in eighths, over at most `kRiverRampVox` (48) of
arc per voxel of drop, never before the previous step, never inside a source
lake's lead. A drop over 2 m stays **hard**: a waterfall. Reaches are now runs
between hard steps; pieces split on a 2-eighth surface deviation as well.

A reach's last piece ends at the cut sample, which is the FIRST sample of the
reach below; it ends there on the reach's **own** surface (the sample before
the cut's T8), never the lower one. (Audit, 2026-10-16: the first cut lerped
to the lower T8, so every waterfall had a pit as deep as the fall over the
last 4 voxels upstream of its cut, the crest standing up out of it at the
upper level, and the deviation test split every reach down to that one
sample. `rivers` B now checks every reach end before a fall against the next
piece's lip.)
`scripts/test_riverbake.mjs` holds the JS port to the engine
(`tests/river_vectors.json`, regenerated by `scripts/gen_river_vectors.py`).

Piece words: `kS_RivSurf` = T8 at end A; `kS_RivLipY` = lip Y (i16) | fall A→B
in eighths (u16).

### 4. `waterApply` (GPU `worldgen.wgsl`, CPU `world.cpp`, per voxel equal)

* channel water = the surface of the **nearest** channel piece claiming the
  column (smallest d2; ties keep the first listed), top cell part-full. NOT the
  lowest: a ramp is cut into short pieces of different slope, a column near a
  bend is claimed by several, and their surfaces there can differ by voxels —
  the lowest stepped the water down wherever a steeper piece's channel edge
  passed (measured: 508 leaking voxels in the `rivers` fixture);
* a piece's **steepness** M = 1 + its fall per column in whole voxels (rounded
  up, from the fall and the packed length);
* a channel column left **dry** (floor at or over its surface: the shallow edge
  of a U) and every **bank** stand at least S + M over every piece within
  `RIVER_LIP` of them, so the wet column next door has a wall, never a gap;
* dams between reaches are gone except at a waterfall, which is two strips past
  its cut: the **CREST** (`RIVER_CREST` = 2 voxels: a sill one cell under the
  upstream surface, that water over it) and the **CURTAIN** (`RIVER_CURTAIN` =
  2: the upstream surface's water standing on the lower reach's floor — a held
  sheet of water against the cliff). Together they fit one 4-voxel bake sample,
  so a mountain river whose falls come one a sample (the default map's
  `river_redrun` has 164) keeps a curtain on every fall;
* banks from the cut to `RIVER_CREST + RIVER_CURTAIN + 2` past it hold the
  upstream surface, so the curtain's corners are walled;
* **the brink** (2026-10-16, ticket 11-08-20): the water rounds over a fall.
  The last `RIVER_DRAW` (6) voxels upstream of a fall's cut sink by
  `brinkDraw8` (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7 eighths toward the cut; distance along the
  piece's B cut normal); the crest's two columns stand `fallDrop8` = 9 and 12
  eighths under the upstream surface (top cell one under it, sill under that),
  the curtain's two 16 and 24 (full to 2 and 3 cells under it). The slope
  steepens 1, 1, 1, 1, 2, 2 | 2, 3, 4, 8 eighths a voxel into the sheet. Only
  the WATER draws down: floors, banks and walls keep the reach's own surface;
* **the curtain's ends** (2026-10-16): a channel column the crest/curtain
  strips cross that is left dry (the shallow edge of a U) holds the fall
  (`lipY`) like the bank beside it; it stood at the lower reach's level + 1,
  and both ends of every curtain on `default` spilled down the cliff for ever.
  `rivers` L walks every waterfall of `default` (CPU twin) for such leaks;
* **another water** (2026-10-16, ticket 11-13-31; `RIVER_FOREIGN` =
  `kRiverWaterfall`): a channel claiming the column whose surface differs from
  the nearest channel's by more than a waterfall plus the nearer piece's
  steepness is another river (or a meander lobe of this one). A lower one adds
  nothing to the floor / steepness / strips (a second walk over the pieces,
  only where it happens); a higher one within `RIVER_LIP` makes the column
  its bank wall. Measured before: `default`'s Merebeck (a trace whose source
  landed on Hollowcrown Beck's channel) ran 36 voxels from it for 20 km and
  the lowest-floor rule stood the upper river's water on the lower river's
  floor: held slabs 66 voxels tall. The map is fixed as well
  (`design_world.mjs` joins a trace to a river it reaches, if that river goes
  where it was sent, and never picks a source on one).

The top cell's missing eighths travel as `LandCol/Col.fluidGap` (zero-init =
full; colCache `CCW_WP` bits 25..27) into `genCellIn`.

### 5. The look (`raymarch.wgsl`)

* `fallingSheet`: a **side face** of water is one of three things
  (`waterSideKind`): a WALL when a solid stands in front (water seen through
  glass or ice: the plain flat face it always was), a RISER when the water
  outside stands less than 0.75 cell below this cell's surface (a step of a
  running surface: lit as top), else a FALLING SHEET — vertical whitewater
  streaks in FREE FALL: advected exactly from the crest (a parcel d metres
  under it has fallen for T(d) = (sqrt(v0² + 2 g d) - v0) / g, v0 = 1 m/s,
  so 4.5 m/s a metre down, 7.7 m/s three metres down; the streaks speed up
  and draw out into ropes toward the foot). `fallCrestDepthM` finds d up the
  face's own column (strides of 4 cells, then cell by cell, capped at
  6.4 m). Was a constant 3.2 m/s (ticket 2026-10-16_11-08-20: "moves
  unrealistically slowly"). A held curtain, a rapid's step and the CA's own
  spill all read as water going down. Water stepping off onto a floor drops
  by its own depth (a puddle's 2/8 edge is not a waterfall).
* WATER IN FLIGHT (air or a gas under the cell) draws the sheet on EVERY
  face: its underside — the face any ray from below meets — and its top as
  end-on churning white water (ticket 2026-10-16_11-18-23: seen from below,
  a falling column was a near-clear pane with only the tops of its cells
  visible through it; a partial cell in free fall was a dark glass cube).
* White water is lit by `whiteWaterLight`: sky by the face's normal (a face
  turned down takes the side-on sky: the sheet is lit through) plus the key
  light, the enclosure's ambient where the sky cannot reach. NOT times
  `mediaLightK`, which the first cut applied on top of those absolute lights
  and so squared the night: every streak of a curtain at night came out
  black. Surface foam (authored colour) IS times `mediaLightK`; it used to
  glow full daylight white at midnight.
* `fallCueAt`: a held curtain (liquid over liquid, air in front, liquid along
  it) throws the waterfall mist and spray, its foot is the plunge. The mist's
  key-light shadow darkens the key light only (it scaled the sky ambient too,
  so mist with the moon down was darker than what it hangs in front of).
* `gSlopeFlow`: the surface's own macro slope is a current (downhill, 9 m/s
  per unit slope, capped at 3 m/s), added to the flow grid where the two
  surface terms that draw motion read it — a held ramp, a rapid and an adopted
  stream run visibly, though nothing in the sim moves.
* **Held steps** (2026-10-17, tickets 11-26-13 / 11-37-20): the raymarch binds
  the still table (render binding 50, read-only; `heldColumnT8` sets
  `gHeldKind` per CA water pixel). A HELD column's side face is a falling sheet
  only over a REAL drop (`heldSideKind`): the water or floor in front stands
  `HELD_SHEET_MIN_DROP` (2.5) cells or more under the column's surface (up to
  two cells of the column over the face counted), or the column in front is
  open for `HELD_SHEET_LOOK` (3) cells down. A frozen spill fan or stream's
  one- and two-cell steps are risers (surface); the bake's curtain (tens of
  voxels), an overhang or a cliff stay sheets. Unheld water keeps the 0.75
  rule above.
* **The frozen stream plays back** (same tickets; the owner: a frozen stream
  "continues to flicker" as it did live): an ADOPTED column's surface is drawn
  at T8 + o(t) eighths, o = its recording in `STILL_ECHO` (two words a column
  after STILL_MOUTH: 8 signed bytes, the live surface minus T8 every 4 ticks,
  -128 = no water; the sim agent's `sim_stream.wgsl` writes it), played back at
  7.5 samples/s from a per-column phase (`ECHO_PHASE_JITTER`), interpolated and
  rounded to whole eighths; with both words 0, a procedural -3..+3 flicker
  (`echoProc`). A map RIVER column (STILL_MOUTH word set) takes the procedural
  flicker scaled by its current (`RIVER_FLICKER_V0`/`V1`, 0.08..0.6 m/s); a
  lake and the sea never flicker. Ticket 11-37-20's scene turned out to be
  MAP river water (all of it `gHeldKind` 1 in a debug-tinted run at its
  camera), not an adopted stream; its tall terraces up the right bank are
  steps of 3+ cells of held map water, still sheets by the rule above. fs re-meets the ray with the column's box at that height
  (`streamSurfaceHit`) and moves the hit, face, water path and top-cell fill;
  a column drawn dry where the ray meets its bed first is not shaded as water.
  `waterNormal` adds the neighbours' drawn offsets to its column gradient (the
  patchwork of per-cell tilts a live stream shows). Render-only; costs the
  tag + map word on every CA water pixel, plus the echo words of the column
  and its four neighbours on an adopted one. Limits: a raised column met only
  past its real water (silhouette, under a cell), and the far field, draw
  the held surface. `HELD_WATER_LOOK` false = the old image.

## Phase 2 — persistent streams become held (`sim_stream.wgsl`, landed)

A breach stream that pools sleeps by itself. One that runs from a held body to
another held body (or the same river downstream) is fed and drained forever:
the conveyor again, now player-made. It is adopted into a **dynamic held
target** once steady, and released when its source is cut.

* **Storage**: the still table grew to FOUR words a window column (worldgen's
  STILL_WORDS): [0] tag, [1] the map's target, [2] a stream's target (T8 low 20
  bits | hop << 20), [3] the stream's observation (surface | age | pending
  action) or, once adopted, its liquid (low 12 bits) | flood count << 12 |
  pending action. `stillLevel8` reads [2]
  where [1] is empty, so the held rule, the reservoir and the renderer need no
  other change. Dense worldgen clears the table (`wg_fill_stillWater`); `cols`
  keeps [2]/[3] while a slot's tag is unchanged.
* **Adoption** (SUPERSEDED for map-fed streams by phase 2b's natural test,
  below; the hop chain, one writer per column and release are as here)
  (`scan` then `commit`, after the CA, over the dirty list; only
  the topmost dirty chunk of each window column acts for it, so one writer per
  column): a column's surface is STEADY within one voxel of where the count
  began for 120 visits, it has OUTFLOW (somewhere lower beside it, or a held
  neighbour at or under it — the body it drains into), and it is FED: a
  neighbour holds a target at or over its surface (the map's water, hop 0, or
  an adopted column, hop h) with an open way into it. It is adopted at its
  surface with hop = smallest h + 1 (max 1023). Hops strictly fall toward the
  map's water, so support never runs in a circle.
* **Release**: an adopted column visited (its chunk woke) and no longer fed at
  its own target by a neighbour of SMALLER hop is released. Plugging a mouth
  puts a wall in its column (refused support) and the cascade runs down the
  stream one tick per column, as the released water flows into the next
  column as water over its target and wakes it.
* **Flooded** (audit, 2026-10-16): an adopted column that has stood OVER its
  target long enough is released -- a leaky count in [3] bits 12..23, +1 a
  visit over, +16 a visit more than a voxel over, -1 a visit at or under,
  released at 4000. The steady test cannot tell a stream from a big basin
  filling at under a voxel per 120 ticks, so such a basin is adopted part way
  up; once it rose past that target, its water -- fed for ever through the
  breach -- ran into the column and back to the reservoir (GIVE) for ever, a
  chunk awake between a source and a sink (rule 2). Now that loop is bounded:
  ~250 visits for a basin a voxel or more over, ~4000 for a shallow one.
  Why not faster: the count is ALSO what the mouth of a stream still being
  adopted does -- the columns at the river's level are adopted first (fed by
  the river) and sink the running stream's water by GIVE until the stream
  above is adopted (measured in `rivers` S: their count climbs one a visit to
  912 by tick 1050, when the last column adopts, then falls). At 120 the S
  stream FLAPPED (22, 45, 27, 15, 69, 12, 112 ... of 153 adopted, release
  cascades); at 4000 it adopts exactly as without the rule (6, 28, 46, 65,
  88, 114, 153). S now fails if the adopted count drops while the stream
  runs, and prints the largest flood count per sample.
* Not saved (derived: a reloaded stream runs as water and re-adopts), and a
  function of the tick stream decided on the GPU in tick order. A LOAD clears
  the dynamic words (`clearStreams`, PT_LOADRESET `lr_streamClear`): a load
  refills the window through the streamed path, whose `cols` keeps a stream
  while its tag is unchanged, so a save loaded where the window stood kept
  the old session's streams (audit, 2026-10-16).
* Cost: `scan` / `commit` find the topmost dirty chunk with one dirtyIn read
  per thread (31 threads, OR-ed into a workgroup word) instead of 31 in a row
  on thread 0, and `scan` stores word [3] only when it changes (every dry
  column of every topmost dirty chunk passes through it each tick).

Measured (`rivers` S: a channel dug from the lake ~12 voxels down to main, 153
columns of its own): 153 wet by tick 300, all 153 adopted by tick 1050, its
chunks 6 -> 0 awake by tick 1350; plugged, 147 released within 150 ticks (the
6 left stand level with main, which supports them as its own).

## Phase 2b — a stream is held only when holding it looks natural (2026-10-17)

Tickets 2026-10-17_11-26-13 (a bank dug through; the water spilling onto flat
ground "solidifies into vertical waterfall column things that stay
solid/sleeping ... the vertical columns rise in an unnatural way for a second
or so, then collapse into a slightly more normal mound shape before building
again as water pours in") and 11-37-20 (a stream dug beside the river "looks
great in how it flows when spawning, but slowly becomes a sheer frozen
waterfall"). The owner's rule: *a stream we make auto-freezes only after a long
period in which it is not carrying water to a new area, and only if what feeds
it is basically infinite (a lake, map water); frozen, it still delivers water
the same way; it freezes only when that looks natural.*

### What phase 2 did

(No run of `stream-natural` on the phase-2 shaders was recorded.) What its
fixtures were built to catch, from the tickets and the first natural cuts: a
flood spreading over flat ground and a basin filling below a stream rose more
slowly than "within a voxel for 120 visits", so their columns were held part
way up at whatever height they stood at that visit (a crest or a trough),
refilled to it at once (the RISE), drowned by the rising water and let go by
the flood count (the COLLAPSE), and held again 120 visits later (the CYCLE);
and water in flight over a step was taken as the surface.

### The natural test (`sim_stream.wgsl` STREAMS ADOPT ONLY WHEN NATURAL)

A column a MAP-fed neighbour would feed (the map's water, hop 0, or a map-fed
held column) is held when ALL of:

* **Fed from the map** — unchanged hop chain; a POOL feeder never counts here.
* **Its own surface is steady.** The surface observed is the RESTING one
  (`restingSurface`): the top of the first run of liquid, walking down, that
  stands on solid or powder. Liquid with air under it is in FLIGHT — over a
  step, off a ledge — and is not the surface (phase 2's `columnSurface` took
  it). The column is watched in windows of `STREAM_SUB` (16) visits: the
  window's MEAN against an ANCHOR (the mean the run began at). A window more
  than `STREAM_MEAN_TOL` (2) eighths off the anchor re-anchors (the anchor follows
  the mean: it is what the column is held at). The run must last
  `sim.streamSteadyTicks` (600 = 20 s) of windows (64 visits each, `STREAM_SUB`)
  in which three visits in four found a resting surface and at most a quarter
  water in flight, and in which the anchor has not DRIFTED more than
  `STREAM_RUN_DRIFT` (4 eighths, half a voxel) from where the run began —
  THE RUN IS A LEVEL, NOT A WINDOW (2026-10-18: every re-anchor used to
  restart the run, and a stream with any churn — `rivers` S, MPM on — never
  had one longer than four windows; its head's anchor wandering either side
  of its place now keeps the run, one rising half a voxel restarts it, which
  is `stream-natural`'s own measure of "still filling").
* **Nothing downstream moved for as long.** Every column keeps the newest fine
  and coarse event stamps among its own and its DOWNSTREAM neighbours' (open
  beside its surface with no water standing higher there), in
  `STREAM_OBS` [2]; `commit` publishes them ([3]) and the neighbours' next
  `scan` reads them, so an event anywhere down a stream reaches its top one
  column a tick later. An event is a column's FIRST wet window (a new area:
  fine and coarse) or its level over a BLOCK of 8 windows moving
  `STREAM_CHAIN_TOL` (4 eighths) against the block before (fine). The map's
  water is a sink that never changes (never scanned, never read). A flood
  still spreading, a basin still filling, keep every column above them live
  CA.
* **It is held at the shape it has.** The target is the column's current
  resting surface, taken only at a visit where that stands within
  `STREAM_AT_TOL` (1) eighth of the anchor: the frozen shape is the mean
  shape, not a crest, and the reservoir has nothing to fill (no RISE).
* **It runs.** A held feeder stands HIGHER, or somewhere beside it is LOWER,
  or its surface moved in the last window. A still pool touching the lake at
  its own level has none of these and sleeps as plain water; phase 2 held it
  (fed by the lake at its level, "draining" into the lake at its level).
* That is the HEAD. **The front does not wait**: a column a held map-fed
  stream column feeds (`fedByHeldStream`; also one the map's water feeds as
  well — the mouth of a stream at the river it runs into, which as "smallest
  feeding hop >= 1" stopped the front 20 columns short of `main` in `rivers`
  S) is taken at once at its anchor, not into a fall, never one let go
  before.
* **The head is the reservoir's edge** (2026-10-18, `sim_step.wgsl`
  stillWant): a held map-fed stream is not refilled from the reservoir —
  except its hop-1 columns, the ones the map's own water feeds. A head under
  the map's surface row cannot take the map's water through the CA (the
  map's cell at its top row is submerged; the one over it would pour over the
  head's target, held, refused); `rivers` S's head was held, stood more than
  a voxel under its target, was released DRAINED and the 75 columns under it
  went for want of support. Refilled, it stands at its target.

RELEASE, besides the old rules (support gone, flooded): a map-fed held column
whose own water stands more than a voxel under its target (DRAINED, per visit:
debounced over a window, a held basin a dug extension drains let go late and
the extension filled at 0.47 of the live rate, `stream-natural` E, measured
2026-10-18) is let go. NOT on news from below (the first cut released on a
coarse stamp newer than the adoption, and let go whenever a cascade's side
lane got wet for the first time).
A released column observes from scratch (a whole window again) and its release
is a FINE event for the columns above it, which then wait a window too: no
adopt/release cycle runs faster than `sim.streamSteadyTicks`. The reason is
counted per column (`STREAM_OBS` [6] releases | adoptions, [7] by reason:
support | flood | event | drained), which is what `stream-natural` D reads.

### Storage (still table, `simulation.cpp` kStillWaterWords)

After the mouth words: **`STILL_ECHO`**, two words a column (render-only:
eight signed-byte samples of the resting surface, every 4 ticks into slot
`(tick / 4) % 8`, relative to the target the column is adopted at, -128 = no
water; written while observed, rebased at adoption, frozen while held, zeroed
on release / a fresh observation / every clear); then **`STREAM_OBS`**, eight
words a column (anchor | run, the window's sums, the stamp scratch and the
published stamps, the tag they belong to, the adoption tick, the counters).
The ECHO WRITER (`echoSample`, `echoShift`) samples on the slot's FIRST tick
only, so the recording is one sample every 4 ticks, 7.5 a second, as the
renderer plays it back; a re-anchor shifts the recorded bytes with the anchor,
the adoption rebases them onto the target. Render-only: no sim kernel reads
the echo, `sim_occupancy` (the world hash) binds no still table, and saves do
not carry it (`worldio`). `stream-natural` G asserts that at least
`streamNat.echoRecordedMin` of the held columns carry a recording and no
sample stands past `streamNat.echoRangeMax` eighths (measured: 354 of 454
held columns recorded, samples -5..+6). `STREAM_OBS` [7] byte 2 (the retired
event release's count) is the observer's run drift while the column is
observed.
Words [0]..[3] of the records are unchanged in meaning; an unadopted [3] is 0
unless `sim.streamPoolRoot` is on (it then holds the conduit's own legacy
observation, as before). Rule 1: everything a `scan` reads of another column
(its published stamps, its tag word) is written only by `commit`, after the
barrier; a `scan` writes only its own column's words.

### Knobs

`sim.streamSteadyTicks` (600, 32..4000) and `sim.streamPoolRoot` (0/1, default
0), both NO_WGSL. They reach `sim_stream.wgsl` through **TickParams'
first padding word after `soluteDrift` (`_padSplash0`)**: low 16 bits the
window, bit 16 the root switch (`support.cpp` SubmitTick). Riding a pad keeps
world.h, common.wgsl and every shader's SPIR-V cache untouched (a WGSL tuning
row is a prelude-wide recompile); rename the field the next time either file
changes anyway.

### What is built (`sim_stream.wgsl` + `sim_step.wgsl` THE CONDUIT)

* **POOL streams.** An adopted column's [3] bit 24 (`STREAM_POOL_BIT` /
  `STILL_POOL_BIT`) says its stream is rooted on a finite source. The still
  table's per-column layout is otherwise unchanged (bits 24..29 were spare;
  the liquid is still [3]'s low 12 bits, the flood count bits 12..23). A
  pending POOL adopt is action 3 (`STREAM_ACT_ADOPT_POOL`): no bit of the
  pending word was free, the action field had one value left.
* **Rooting** (`scan`, unadopted branch). A steady column with outflow whose
  held feeders fail may be fed by a LIVE SOURCE (`liveSource(.., true)`): an
  UNHELD lateral neighbour whose water stands `STREAM_POUR` (2, the CA's own
  equalize threshold) or more eighths over it, that can actually pour
  (`sideSurface`: its top cell can split, or there is water under it, so a
  stranded one-eighth film is not a source), with an open way in, and that is
  a ROOT (`isRoot`): nothing stands `STREAM_POUR` over IT beside it, no held
  column beside it has a target at or over its surface (a column a held body
  can feed is that body's), and it is a POOL -- `STREAM_ROOT_DEPTH` (3) cells
  of the liquid deep (`sourceDeep`). Without the depth, the flat stretches of
  a shallow stream on a gentle slope all passed as roots: measured on `rivers`
  S, 131 of the lake-fed stream's 152 adopted columns came out POOL, so the
  map stream ran as a conduit (4 of 6 chunks awake) and its plugged leftover
  was re-adopted and held (123 of 153 columns 900 ticks on). A root also
  needs `STREAM_ADOPT_TICKS_ROOT` (240) steady visits, twice the map's, so a
  map chain coming down the same water is there first (S again: 78 of the
  first 87 adopted columns were POOL, rooted on the deep flat top of the
  lake-fed channel). And every POOL candidate needs a STRICT outflow --
  somewhere 2 eighths lower or air, a held neighbour only if its target is 2
  eighths under: a flat pool beside a held column at its own level otherwise
  has both a feed and an outflow, and the short window flood-filled the
  source from the head (measured, `stream-pool`: 319 reservoir columns
  adopted, the source frozen, the conduit stalled at the cap). And a root is
  WIDE (`sourceWide`): 29 of the 48 cells round it at its surface row hold the
  liquid -- depth did not tell a pool from the deep, flat top of a channel a
  lake feeds through water not yet adopted (S: 56 of 120 adopted columns POOL
  at tick 600, discharging into the map chain beside them), and NOT A SINK: no
  POOL stream's column among those 48 (the mound under a POOL mouth's
  waterfall is deep and wide; rooted, the basin became a second conduit fed by
  the first, ~300 basin columns, churning the ledger to its cap and holding the
  real head back). And A FALL ENDS A
  CONDUIT: a POOL feeder counts only within a voxel over the candidate
  (`streamSupportTo`), so the basin under a waterfall at a POOL mouth is never
  adopted (it was: ~105 basin columns, the mouth emitting into its own held
  sink, the conduit stalled at the cap). Every
  column of a running stream is fed by the unheld column upstream of it; only
  the top of the slope (the pool's edge, beside the pool at its own level) is
  fed by one nothing higher feeds. So POOL adoption starts at the source with
  hop 1 and walks down exactly as a map stream does (hop h + 1 from a held
  feeder of hop h; a POOL feeder makes a POOL column; an equal hop prefers the
  map's feeder). A column fed by a held POOL column needs only
  `STREAM_ADOPT_TICKS_POOL` (24) steady visits, not 120: the ledger refills an
  adopted column to its target whatever it stood at, and the long window left
  the thin, flickering tail of a finite stream unadopted for good (the first
  diagnosed run: the last ~15 of 80 columns never adopted, their gaps kept the
  middle awake). A root keeps the 120.
* **RETARGET** (`scan` adopted branch, `lowerFeed`; `commit`): a POOL column
  whose feed now stands LOWER -- the pool it drains has fallen, or the column
  above it was lowered -- has its target lowered to that level (at most
  `STREAM_RETARGET_MAX`, a voxel, a step; the excess it then stands over is
  deposited) instead of being released. Released, its water drained into the
  next column, which lost its feed too: the whole stream let go from the top
  every time the source fell two eighths and re-adopted 120 visits later
  (measured on `stream-pool`: 209, 134, 99, 15, 49, 115, 205 ... columns
  adopted, the middle awake through every cycle). The pending word keeps the
  liquid and POOL bit where neighbours read them; the drop rides in the flood
  bits (action 3 on an adopted column). Targets only ever fall: bounded.
* **Release with hysteresis** (`scan`, adopted branch). A POOL column with no
  held feeder of smaller hop stays held while ANY unheld neighbour still
  pours into it (`liveSource(.., false)`, no root test). So when the source
  falls below the head's target the head is released, its own water (unheld
  now) still pours into the column below, which is the head from then on, and
  the held stream RETREATS from the top as the source runs out instead of
  cascading. Plus STARVED: a POOL column under its target while the ledger
  snapshot is empty counts +1 a visit on the flood count, so a mouth waiting
  on a source that stopped pouring is let go in bounded time.
* **The ledger** (`sim_step.wgsl`). Two words after the column records
  (`STILL_LEDGER` = `WORLD_N * WORLD_N * STILL_WORDS`: [0] the live balance in
  eighths, i32; [1] its snapshot; the buffer grew by four words). The CA adds
  through binding 62 (`stillLedger`, the SAME buffer as 58, bound again as
  atomics so the table's hot reads stay plain loads); `sim_stream.wgsl
  commit`'s workgroup 0 copies live -> snapshot after the CA, every tick the
  CA ran.
  * DEPOSIT: a free-surface cell of a POOL column OVER its target gives ALL its
    excess, unrolled, at STAGE 0 (beside the TAKE). Left to stage 5 the excess
    split into the next held column's air first and ran down the whole stream.
    Refused while the snapshot is at `STILL_LEDGER_CAP` (512 eighths); then
    the head's over-target cells count as AT REST (`heldRest`), so the head
    holds the source back instead of spilling down the stream (CAPPED).
  * WITHDRAW: TAKE / GROW of a POOL cell refill from the ledger, refused while
    the snapshot is <= 0. A POOL TAKE does NOT spend the visit: the refilled
    cell goes on and makes the move it would have made (the mouth emits at
    the rate the CA gave it before it was held, not half of it).
  * NO FLOW INSIDE A CONDUIT (`heldBlocks`, `HELD_REST_POOL`): a cell at rest
    in a POOL column never pours into another POOL column, even one under its
    target -- the ledger refills that one. Measured without it: the ledger
    turned over ~19 eighths a tick against a real throughput of ~3.5, a
    conveyor of refill-and-pour INSIDE the held stream that kept the middle
    awake. The one exception is a cell holding none of the liquid (a column
    gone DRY has no cell to take a refill; refused its neighbour's water it
    cut the conduit, measured: five dry adopted columns stopped the stream).
  * STARVED, part 1 (`heldBlocks` / `heldBlocksAt`): while the snapshot is
    empty a POOL column under its target is FULL to any held neighbour at
    rest, so the deficit stays at the mouth instead of running up the stream.
  * STARVED, part 2 — the CLOSED VALVE (`stepLiquid` after stage 1b): a cell at
    rest in a POOL column while the snapshot is empty moves nothing sideways
    or diagonally; it keeps its chunk awake (`DIRTY_R_REACT`) while it has
    somewhere to go or a refill it wants. Without it the mouth would spill its
    own column empty, an empty column has no liquid cell to take the refill,
    and nothing would wake it when the ledger came back: the mouth would sleep
    while the head filled the ledger to the cap. The middle has nowhere to go
    (its neighbours are held and full, or starved and so full) and sleeps.
  * `canFlowAnywhere` and the valve ask ONE function, `liquidSideWork`
    (stages 2-4, split out of the mirror), so they cannot drift.
* **WAKE ON RELEASE** (`commit`): a released column's chunk is marked for the
  next tick, so the columns that rested on it are looked at again even when
  the released water does not move. Once per adoption.
* **A REGEN CLEARS THE TABLE** (`SubmitWorldgen`, paged path). The dense path
  always cleared it (`wg_fill_stillWater`, `C_DENSEWG`), but the paged path
  never ran that row and `cols` keeps a stream while its column's tag is
  unchanged -- which a regeneration of the same window always is. So a paged
  regen kept the old world's streams (a phase-2 bug) and now the ledger's
  balance: `stream-pool` E caught it (two runs of the same pour from fresh
  worldgens differed from the first sample; the control with the feature off
  did not). The whole table is zeroed by a WriteBuffer before the regen.
* **Map-rooted streams are unchanged**: the infinite reservoir, the rolled
  GIVE, no ledger, the 120-visit window. Every new branch is behind
  `stillPool` (a POOL record).
* **Still-table layout**: per-column words unchanged and backward compatible
  (the POOL flag is [3] bit 24, previously always 0; a reader masking the
  liquid with `& 0xFFF` and the flood count with `>> 12 & 4095` is
  unaffected); the ledger is four words AFTER the column records, so any
  reader indexing by `stillIndex` never sees them.

### Determinism, termination, mass

* Rule 1: deposits and withdrawals are `atomicAdd` / `atomicSub` whose
  results are never read (order-free integer sums). Every decision — the
  refusals, `heldBlocks`' starved rule, the valve, `scan`'s starved count —
  reads the SNAPSHOT, written once per tick by one thread after the barrier
  that ends the CA rows. One writer per column for the table, as before.
* Rule 2: the new writes are a deposit (moves a POOL surface DOWN to its
  target) and a withdrawal (UP to it); both stop at the target, and every
  eighth withdrawn was deposited. The source loses water monotonically
  (nothing pours back into it from a lower held column), so it stops pouring
  in finite time; then the head is released, the retreat runs to the mouth,
  and every column is ordinary water that settles. Hard bounds where that
  argument has holes: the flood count (an over-target column — a capped
  head) and the starved count (a mouth waiting on an empty ledger), both
  STREAM_FLOOD_COUNT visits.
* MASS: voxels + MPM particles + the live ledger is EXACT (each write moves
  the eighths it adds to or takes from the ledger). What can be STRANDED in
  the ledger when no POOL column is left: at most the cap plus one tick's
  deposits, and at least minus one tick's withdrawals (each cell decides on
  the snapshot, not on what the others took: the OVERDRAFT). A load clears it
  (`clearStreams`), a dense worldgen clears it (the Fill); a window shift
  that drops the columns strands it. All bounded by the cap.
* ONE GLOBAL LEDGER for the window (the approximation): two finite-source
  streams running at once share it, so water poured into one can leave by
  the other's mouth (never more than the cap in transit). Per-stream ledgers
  need a stream identity no column holds today (the hop chain carries no root
  id, and a head several columns wide is several roots).

* **A CONDUIT DISCHARGES INTO MAP WATER** (`heldBlocks`): a cell at rest in a
  POOL column is never refused by a NON-POOL held column; the map's reservoir
  takes the excess back (GIVE). Refused, a finite stream that ran into a river
  could never empty its ledger and stood held for good -- measured on
  `rivers` S: the plugged channel's leftover water was re-adopted as a POOL
  stream draining into main, and 121 of 153 columns were still held 900 ticks
  after the plug (the gate wants <= 20%). And the mirror REFUSAL: a cell at
  rest in a non-POOL held column never pours into a POOL column. With the
  permission alone, a POOL segment between two map-rooted runs of one stream
  was refilled by the map above and discharged into the map below -- a
  source-and-sink conveyor (measured, `rivers` S running: 4 of 6 chunks awake,
  the flood count climbing). Refused, such a segment is fed only by the ledger
  and unheld water, so it starves and rests.

### Known limits

* A basin that rises to the mouth (drowned): the mouth cannot emit, the ledger
  fills to the cap, the head holds the source back and waits, awake, until the
  flood count releases it (bounded: STREAM_FLOOD_COUNT visits, the capped head
  counting +1 a visit). Up to the cap is stranded in the ledger.
* A pour into map water empties into the map's reservoir at the reservoir's
  rolled-GIVE rate, not the CA's: slower than the same water running into an
  unheld basin.
